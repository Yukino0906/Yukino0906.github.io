Cleanroom fabrication and electrical test

A post-CMOS test chip needs both a patterned device and a way to reach it electrically. During my Purdue SURF research at NanoX, I designed an RRAM test array and independently built a measurement platform for emerging-device experiments. Working through the process flow helped me connect the features on a die to the contacts, instruments and software needed to test them.

Preparing a device for probing

  1. Pattern and deposit. I trained in resist coating and development, maskless direct-write lithography on the Heidelberg MLA150, sputtering and pulsed laser deposition. In planning the post-CMOS process, I followed how successive patterning and etching steps define the device while keeping metal pads accessible for probing. My atomic layer deposition training is ongoing.
  2. Inspect the film and contacts. My characterization training covered optical microscopy, ellipsometry and X-ray diffraction. These methods address different questions about patterned features, film thickness and crystal structure. I also trained on a vacuum probe station and on SMU and PMU instrumentation for electrical tests.
  3. Configure the experiment. I built a modular Python framework for the NI PXIe platform. A test recipe specifies the instruments, pin mapping, stimulus and limits. The same execution framework can exercise a device model or drive the measurement hardware and record the results.

Checking the measurement path on hardware

I started with a resistor so that I could compare the acquired values with a known nominal value before applying a device-specific sequence. Ten repeated readings through the physical SMU path clustered around 224 Ω for a nominal 220 Ω reference. The plot shows both the repeatability of the readout and its offset from the nominal value.

I also exercised three device-specific sequences in simulation: bipolar RRAM voltage sweeps, pulse-based conductance updates and digital timing for a stochastic signal. These examples let me develop the test recipes while the hardware validation focused on the resistance path.

Research setting

I carried out this work at Purdue’s NanoX Lab under the guidance of Prof. Haitong Li and Zeshu Wang and received SURF with Distinction in summer 2026.
