Thin-film growth and device function

A memory element should retain a resistance state after programming. Its selector should conduct when addressed and return to a low-conductance state when the bias is removed. In my independent 1S1R study in Wang’s Thin Film Group, I investigated what happens to those functions when the films are grown together in a vertical stack.

Growing and comparing test structures

I deposited oxide thin films by pulsed laser deposition, varied growth conditions, and compared electrical switching across specimens. I studied individual films alongside integrated structures so I could ask where a change in behavior first appeared. X-ray diffraction helped me examine film growth, and cross-sectional microscopy let me inspect the buried interfaces.

Relating interfaces to switching

One challenge was interpreting a sample that switched but behaved like a memory element where I expected a selector response. I examined interface quality in cross-section and tested how current responded as I applied and removed the bias. Temperature-controlled electrical characterization is a further step toward understanding the switching mechanism.

Broader thin-film studies

My materials work also spans ferroelectric and ferromagnetic heterostructures, vanadium dioxide phase-change films, and devices with two-dimensional barriers. Across these systems, I use diffraction, microscopy, and electrical measurements to examine how growth, morphology, and interfaces affect device properties.
